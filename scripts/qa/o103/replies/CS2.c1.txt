The water cycle is how water moves between the oceans, the atmosphere and the land. The Sun heats water so it evaporates as water vapour, and plants release vapour through transpiration; together this is called evapotranspiration. Rising air cools and the vapour condenses around dust or salt into clouds. When droplets collide and grow, they fall as precipitation: rain, snow, sleet or hail depending on the temperature. Some water runs off into streams and rivers and back to the sea. Some soaks in through infiltration and becomes groundwater, which can stay underground for thousands of years. Glaciers and ice caps store water as snow and ice. About 97 percent of Earth's water is salt water, and only about 3 percent is fresh, mostly frozen. Humans affect the cycle through deforestation, which reduces transpiration, and climate change, which speeds evaporation and brings heavier rain in some regions and drought in others.
